Simulations reveal how a solar vortex and reconnecting magnetic fields can launch a rotating jet

Solar spicules are slender jets commonly found in the chromosphere. They lift relatively cool plasma from the lower solar atmosphere, carry energy upward, and in some cases rotate as they evolve. Small-scale vortices also occur frequently between convection-driven granules in the photosphere. Two questions remain incompletely answered: how these photospheric vortices help form spicules, and how much mass and energy the rising jets carry into the corona.
